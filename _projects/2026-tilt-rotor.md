  <section class="tr-section">
    <p class="tr-kicker">01 / OVERVIEW</p>
    <h2>Engineering Challenge</h2>
    <p>Our 35-lb hybrid eVTOL aircraft requires a tilt-rotor mechanism that transitions the motors between vertical and horizontal flight. I owned the mechanical development of the system, including dynamic load modeling, actuator selection and validation, structural analysis, manufacturing, assembly, and testing.</p>
    <div class="tr-callout"><strong>Design objective</strong><p>Build a rigid, manufacturable mechanism that supports demanding transition loads while integrating with ArduPilot-based flight controls.</p></div>
  </section>

  <section class="tr-section">
    <p class="tr-kicker">02 / ANALYSIS</p>
    <h2>Dynamics and Load Modeling</h2>
    <p>I modeled the gyroscopic and inertial loads generated by aircraft motion and rotor tilting. Some analyzed operating cases produced gyroscopic moments exceeding 6.5 N·m, informing the load cases used to size and evaluate the bracket and shaft.</p>
    <div class="tr-equation-box">
  <p class="tr-kicker">GOVERNING EQUATION</p>

  <div class="tr-equation">
    \[
    \sum \mathbf{M}_Q =
    \mathbf{I}_Q\boldsymbol{\alpha}
    + \boldsymbol{\omega}\times
    (\mathbf{I}_Q\boldsymbol{\omega})
    - m\mathbf{r}_{QG}\times\mathbf{a}_Q
    \]
  </div>

  <p>
    The rigid-body moment equation was used to calculate
    applied loads about the accelerating tilt pivot Q,
    accounting for angular acceleration, rotational
    coupling, and pivot acceleration.
  </p>
</div>
    <div class="tr-grid">
      <figure class="tr-figure"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/tilt-rotors-gallery/figure-11-16-dynamics.png' | relative_url }}" alt="Tilt-rotor free-body diagram with body axes and applied loads" loading="lazy"><figcaption>Free-body diagram and coordinate definitions.</figcaption></figure>
    </div>
    <div class="tr-grid">
</div>
    <p><strong>Engineering approach:</strong> rigid-body moment balance about the tilt pivot, including angular acceleration, gyroscopic coupling, and acceleration of the reference point. I am developing a MATLAB tool to evaluate revised geometries and vectored-yaw requirements more efficiently.</p>
  </section>

<h3>MATLAB Dynamics Implementation</h3>

<p>
  I developed a MATLAB model to calculate the moments
  acting on the tilt-rotor assembly about its pivot point,
  accounting for angular acceleration, rotational coupling,
  and acceleration of the pivot.
</p>

<details class="tr-code-details">
  <summary>View MATLAB Dynamics Code</summary>

  <div class="tr-code-content">
    <pre><code>
M_Q_B = T_total + omega_cross_B*h_total_Q - m_G*r_QG_cross_B*a_QO_B

%% 8. Transfer moment from Q to P

% Position of P relative to Q, expressed in B
r_QP_B = [0; h; 0];

% Net external force on the system
% Must be calculated from center-of-mass acceleration
F_net_B = m_G * a_QO_B;

% Transfer moment from Q to P
M_P_B = M_Q_B - cross(r_QP_B, F_net_B);

disp('Moment about P [N*m]:');
disp(M_P_B);
    </code></pre>
  </div>
</details>

  <section class="tr-section">
    <p class="tr-kicker">03 / VALIDATION</p>
    <h2>CAD and Structural Analysis</h2>
    <p>During the first iteration of the design, our system sustained unexpected loads and crashed. I evaluated bracket stress using ANSYS Static Structural to determine the required load that occured to result in a failed test flight. </p>
    <figure class="tr-figure"><img src="{{ '/assets/images/tilt-rotors-gallery/ansys-load-to-induce-yield.png' | relative_url }}" alt="ANSYS equivalent stress result for the tilt-rotor bracket" loading="lazy"><figcaption>ANSYS equivalent-stress results required to induce yield during crash analysis.</figcaption></figure>
    <p>A redesign included doubling the bracket thickness and adding a stainless-steel retaining plate to prevent gears from demeshing.</p>
  </section>

  <section class="tr-section">
    <p class="tr-kicker">05 / ITERATION</p>
    <h2>Testing and Redesign</h2>
    <p>I developed test procedures to assess transition speed at varying thrust levels. Following flight testing, I designed and manufactured two revised components in under two weeks to improve rigidity and support faster transitions compatible with our flight-control requirements. I'm currently working on a new design that incorporates vectored yaw. A redesign requires lengthening the bracket to allow for backwards tilt.</p>
